# Study 8E Provenance, Independence, and Cross-Study Integrity Plan

**Experiment:** `S8E-ECTV-001`

## Source independence

External sources are not automatically independent because they have different names or hosting organizations.

### SatNOGS and OPS-SAT

ESA explicitly states that SatNOGS and the amateur-radio community contributed to the OPS-SAT-1 re-entry reception campaign. Therefore:

- OPS-SAT-1 is a secondary dependent source;
- it may corroborate reception-density/gap behavior;
- it may not be counted as an independent replication of SatNOGS;
- no combined SatNOGS + OPS-SAT sample size or pooled inferential population is permitted.

### LENS

LENS is separately collected Starlink-network measurement evidence. It is independent in collection lineage from the planned SatNOGS/OPS-SAT timing sources, but it represents a different network architecture. Independence of collection does not establish domain equivalence.

### HDTN

HDTN is software and a later implementation harness. It is not an external empirical population.

## Cross-study reuse

Study 8E may reuse **methods** from other repository studies when the reuse is explicit and versioned.

### Permitted reuse

From Study 8:

- exact standardized cryptographic-object definitions and byte counts;
- policy/state-machine semantics;
- disruption definitions, only after extension-specific event-ordering tests;
- claim-boundary language as an inherited minimum.

From Study 9:

- dataset/source selection before endpoint inspection;
- exact artifact identity and hash binding;
- schema manifest before semantic mapping;
- no imputation of unavailable state;
- explicit protocol-deviation handling;
- separate finite source populations;
- independent reconstruction/audit.

### Prohibited reuse

Study 8E must not import another study's:

- observations as if generated by Study 8E;
- endpoint values as Study 8E results;
- attack/scenario labels as operational recovery truth;
- frozen population denominator;
- statistical conclusions;
- external-validation claims.

## Protocol-deviation rule

A deviation is required when source reality conflicts with a frozen expectation and the conflict could change eligibility, mapping, or endpoints.

A deviation must:

1. preserve the original protocol text;
2. identify when the issue was discovered;
3. state whether endpoint values had already been inspected;
4. prohibit silent repair;
5. define whether the affected source/field becomes ambiguous, remains descriptive only, or is excluded;
6. be committed before continued endpoint computation.

A deviation may not be used to optimize the result after inspection.
