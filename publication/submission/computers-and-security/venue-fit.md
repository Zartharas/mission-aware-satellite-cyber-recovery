# Venue Fit and Backup Strategy

## Primary: Computers & Security (Elsevier)

### Why the paper fits

The journal describes itself as combining leading-edge information-security research with practical security advice for industry, commerce, and academia. The manuscript's core contribution is an empirical security-response/recovery comparison rather than cryptology, and therefore sits within that applied-security framing.

The topic also has direct venue precedent. Recent Computers & Security papers include:

- **Assessing the attack surface of space organizations: A data-driven analysis**, Vol. 164 (2026), 104848, DOI 10.1016/j.cose.2026.104848 — direct space-sector cybersecurity precedent.
- **Securing SatCom user segment: A study on cybersecurity challenges in view of IRIS2**, Vol. 140 (2024), 103799, DOI 10.1016/j.cose.2024.103799 — direct satellite-communications cybersecurity precedent.
- **SCASS: Breaking into SCADA Systems Security**, Vol. 151 (2025), 104315, DOI 10.1016/j.cose.2025.104315 — open, modular cyber-physical security-testbed precedent.
- **LTL-based runtime verification framework for cyber-attack anomaly prediction in cyber-physical systems**, Vol. 155 (2025), 104455, DOI 10.1016/j.cose.2025.104455 — experimental CPS/testbed precedent that includes CubeSat communication data.
- **Intrusion response systems for cyber-physical systems: A comprehensive survey**, Vol. 124 (2023), 102984, DOI 10.1016/j.cose.2022.102984 — intrusion-response and resilient-CPS adjacency.

The present manuscript is differentiated from those works by its controlled comparison of response/recovery policies under common satellite conditions, separate mission/security/recovery outcomes, explicit contact/evidence manipulations, evidence-qualified trusted recovery, condition-specific Pareto analysis, and integrity-frozen reproducibility record.

### Desk-review positioning

Lead with the **security decision and response/recovery problem**, not with satellite engineering alone. The satellite environment matters because intermittent contact, mission-state constraints, and trust evidence create security-response trade-offs; the contribution is a controlled cybersecurity evaluation method with a satellite-specific instantiation.

Avoid positioning the paper as:

- a new Mission Aware theory;
- a satellite threat taxonomy;
- a flight-autonomy certification study;
- a real-RF or operational-spacecraft experiment;
- a universal-autonomy superiority result.

### Primary submission risk

The paper is domain-specific and uses a software-in-the-loop testbed. A desk editor could judge it as more aerospace-systems than general information security. The cover letter and abstract should therefore foreground the transferable cyber-response question, controlled comparative method, practical response/recovery implications, and reproducibility package.

## Backup 1: AIAA Journal of Aerospace Information Systems

Use this target if Computers & Security rejects primarily for venue/domain fit rather than scientific quality. JAIS explicitly covers aerospace computing, information, networks, mission assurance, and aerospace-specific information-system applications. It is a natural home if reviewers/editors want the contribution framed more strongly around spacecraft information systems, autonomy, and mission assurance.

Backup-positioning change: emphasize aerospace mission assurance, contact-constrained onboard/ground response, cFS/NOS3/Fortytwo integration, and the implications for trusted spacecraft recovery. Preserve all security-outcome and responsible-research limitations.

## Backup 2: IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems

Use as the higher-bar aerospace-systems alternative if the manuscript can be framed around generalizable aerospace/electronic-system methodology and the experimental rigor is considered sufficient. The journal covers complex systems for space, command/control, information systems, and mission-related electronic systems.

Backup-positioning change: strengthen systems methodology, experimental design, statistical modeling, and generalizability across command/control contexts. Do not inflate the current SIL evidence into flight validation.

## Decision rule

1. Submit to Computers & Security first after final author attestations and live-guide checks.
2. If desk-rejected for scope, retarget promptly to JAIS without changing scientific results.
3. If a review outcome suggests stronger aerospace-systems framing and the contribution remains methodologically competitive, consider IEEE TAES.

A rejection must never trigger post-hoc changes to the frozen 720-observation population or reinterpretation of P1/P3/P4/P5 beyond the evidence-locked manuscript controls.
