# Cover Letter Draft — Computers & Security

Dear Editors of *Computers & Security*,

I am submitting the manuscript **“Mission-Aware Satellite Cyber Response and Trusted Recovery Under Contact and Evidence Constraints: A Controlled Software-in-the-Loop Study”** for consideration as a **Full Length Article**.

The paper addresses a practical cyber-resilience problem for satellite systems: a defensive action can suppress unauthorized activity yet still impose mission, command-availability, or recovery costs, and nominal operation alone does not establish that recovery is trustworthy. Rather than propose a new threat taxonomy or another anomaly detector, the study compares response and recovery policies under one frozen experimental design while measuring security containment, mission continuity, safety constraints, command availability, and evidence-qualified trusted recovery separately.

The controlled NOS3/Fortytwo/cFS software-in-the-loop campaign produced 720 VALID observations across 24 frozen cells and 30 reproducible seed blocks. The results are deliberately mixed rather than promotional. Mission-state dependence was not supported on the predeclared P1 primary outcomes; modeled missed contact materially delayed the ground-authorized response path; degraded policy-visible evidence changed mission-aware selection and trusted-recovery outcomes; and multi-objective Pareto relations depended on the evaluated condition. The article therefore does not claim universal superiority for autonomous response.

The mission-aware P7 mechanism is a **frozen deterministic rule-based selector**, not an AI/ML decision model. The manuscript therefore does not depend on AI/ML as a scientific security mechanism; this distinction is relevant to the journal's current scope. Any use of generative AI during post-campaign reproducibility-code reconstruction or manuscript preparation is separately and transparently disclosed under Elsevier's publication policy.

I believe the work fits *Computers & Security* because it combines a concrete cybersecurity response problem, controlled cyber-physical experimentation, practical response/recovery implications, and a public reproducibility record. The journal has also recently published space-sector cybersecurity, satellite intrusion-detection, and cyber-physical security-testbed research, making this study relevant to its applied-security readership. The manuscript's contribution is downstream of detection: it evaluates the security, mission-availability, and trusted-recovery consequences of alternative response policies under constrained connectivity and imperfect policy-visible evidence.

The raw frozen campaign and publication-grade integrity material are publicly archived on Zenodo (version DOI **10.5281/zenodo.22181540**; concept DOI **10.5281/zenodo.22181539**). The public code repository is https://github.com/Zartharas/mission-aware-satellite-cyber-recovery, and the reproducibility-hardened snapshot used for submission preparation is commit `99892bd9bb0828bdb3d0a28caf40dbc18fcbc4dc`. The original historical WP10 analysis source was not preserved; the repository clearly labels the subsequently reconstructed statistical code as a post-publication reproducibility implementation and regression-validates it against preserved reference outputs.

The reported experiment used no human participants, no operational spacecraft or ground station, no RF transmission or interference, no operational credentials, and no classified or proprietary mission telemetry. The contact condition is modeled, and the paper makes no flightworthiness or certification claim.

For transparency, the broader research program developed from my doctoral work, whose dissertation manuscript is archived through ProQuest. The journal article reports the controlled experiment and reproducibility record described above; the dissertation relationship is disclosed rather than treated as undisclosed prior dissemination. Elsevier's general journal policy does not treat an academic thesis as prior publication.
