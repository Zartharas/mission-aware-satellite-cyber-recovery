# Concise Abstract Candidate — Computers & Security

**Status:** target-specific candidate. The authoritative abstract remains `../../manuscript/00-title-abstract.md`; adopt this version only if preferred by the live submission guide/portal.

Satellite cyber response must balance containment with mission continuity, command availability, and recovery supported by trustworthy evidence. We report two separately frozen software-in-the-loop studies of deterministic rule-based response/recovery policies; their statistical populations are not pooled. Study 1 comprised 720 VALID observations across 24 frozen cells and evaluated mission state, modeled contact, evidence sufficiency, trusted recovery, and condition-specific multi-objective policy trade-offs. Mission-state dependence was not supported on the predeclared primary outcomes, while modeled missed contact delayed the ground-authorized path and reduced policy-visible evidence changed response selection and trusted-recovery outcomes.

Study 2 prospectively extended the design to adversarial evidence conditions, multiple contact regimes, context ablations, and bounded producer compromise. Its 85-cell campaign produced 3,872 VALID observations with 0 INVALID attempts. Under bounded producer compromise, policy-visible evidence could remain authenticated/current enough for evidence-qualified recovery while being false relative to research-only adjudication truth, showing that evidence qualification is not equivalent to objective correctness. Contact/authorization results exposed policy-specific safety/recovery trade-offs, and context ablations identified specific dependencies rather than a universal dominant variable. The 54 benign/adversarial Block-C contrasts were all zero, but these are a structural label-invariance control because the frozen cause label does not change hidden truth or generated policy-visible evidence; they are not empirical evidence of benign-versus-adversarial causal discrimination. Independent reproduction of the Study-2 Phase-7 result tables found zero mismatches.

Together, the studies support a bounded conclusion: satellite cyber-response value depends on evidence integrity, authorization/contact constraints, and policy context. The work does not support a global policy ranking, operational flight-readiness claim, or AI/ML-based response claim.
