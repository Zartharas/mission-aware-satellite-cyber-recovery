# Cover Letter - Journal of Aerospace Information Systems

Dear Editors of the *Journal of Aerospace Information Systems*,

Please consider my manuscript, **“Satellite Cyber Response and Trusted Recovery Under Contact and Adversarial Evidence Constraints,”** for publication as a **Full Paper**.

The paper asks a focused aerospace cybersecurity question: once a cyber-relevant condition has been established, how should a satellite response policy balance containment, mission continuity, contact constraints, command authorization, and the trustworthiness of recovery evidence? Those factors are treated as explicit experimental conditions rather than background assumptions.

The manuscript reports two separately frozen software-in-the-loop studies, analyzed as distinct statistical populations. Study 1 contains 720 VALID observations across 24 frozen cells. It evaluates mission state, modeled contact, evidence sufficiency, trusted recovery, and condition-specific policy trade-offs. Mission-state dependence was not demonstrated on the predeclared primary outcomes. Modeled missed contact delayed the ground-authorized response path, and reduced policy-visible evidence changed trusted-recovery behavior. The resulting comparisons show benefit, equivalence, and disadvantage across conditions rather than a universal policy ranking.

Study 2 contains 3,872 VALID observations across 85 frozen cells with zero INVALID attempts. It extends the design to omitted, stale or replayed, contradictory, and manipulated evidence; bounded producer compromise; multiple modeled contact regimes; context ablations; and adversary-budget stress. Under bounded producer compromise, evidence could satisfy policy-visible authentication and freshness requirements while still being false relative to research-only adjudication truth. Contact and authorization conditions also produced policy-specific safety and recovery trade-offs. A separate implementation independently recomputed the frozen Study 2 numerical result families with zero mismatches.

I believe the work is a good fit for JAIS because the aerospace setting is integral to the research design. Intermittent contact, command authority, mission continuity, software recovery, evidence integrity, and constrained recovery opportunities directly shape the evaluated behavior. The paper is therefore positioned as an aerospace information-systems, cyber-resilience, and mission-assurance study. It does not claim operational flight validation, radio-frequency performance, certification, or universal policy superiority.

The paper is intentionally conservative about its limits. The Study 2 benign/adversarial ambiguity block is treated as a structural label-invariance control because the paired labels do not change hidden truth or generated policy-visible evidence within a family. Study 2 timing values are deterministic logical software time, not measured spacecraft, network, operator, orbital, or radio-frequency latency. The two study populations are not pooled.
