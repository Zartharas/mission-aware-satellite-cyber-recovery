## Why JAIS fits the two-study article

Current AIAA scope describes JAIS as publishing original archival research on aerospace computing, information, networks and communication systems addressing aerospace-specific problems. Listed areas include aerospace systems and software engineering, verification and validation of embedded systems, autonomous systems, systems engineering, and safety and mission assurance.

Paper 1 directly intersects those areas. It evaluates deterministic satellite cyber-response and trusted-recovery policies under explicit software, mission, authorization/contact, evidence-integrity, and adversarial constraints. The central problem is not merely generic cybersecurity applied to a satellite label: intermittent contact, command authority, mission continuity, recovery opportunity, and evidence availability materially determine the modeled system behavior.

The two frozen studies provide quantitative controlled evidence:

- Study 1: 720 VALID observations across 24 frozen cells.
- Study 2: 3,872 VALID observations across 85 frozen cells with 0 INVALID attempts.

The two populations remain separate and are not pooled.

## Recommended JAIS positioning

Lead with the aerospace information-system and mission-assurance decision problem:

- post-detection response and recovery under intermittent contact;
- evidence integrity and evidence sufficiency as recovery constraints;
- command/authorization availability as an aerospace operational dependency;
- explicit separation of containment, evidence-qualified recovery, objective correctness, mission availability, and command availability;
- software-in-the-loop verification/validation with frozen protocols and provenance;
- deterministic rule-based policy comparison rather than learned autonomy;
- preservation of null, equivalent, adverse, and conditional findings rather than global policy ranking.

The satellite environment should remain technically central while the cybersecurity contribution remains explicit.

## Reviewer-sensitive scientific boundaries

### Study-1 mission-state result

Mission-state dependence was not demonstrated on the predeclared primary outcomes. The null result is retained.

### Modeled contact

Modeled missed contact affected authorization-dependent response/recovery timing under the frozen model. It is not real communications latency.

### Evidence qualification versus correctness

Under bounded Study-2 producer compromise, policy-visible evidence can satisfy the frozen evidence-qualification mechanism while remaining false relative to research-only adjudication truth. Evidence qualification must not be represented as an objective correctness oracle.

### Study-2 structural ambiguity control

The benign/adversarial Block-C zero contrasts are a structural label-invariance/control result because the frozen cause label does not alter hidden truth or generated policy-visible evidence within each ambiguity family. They do not establish broad causal-discrimination capability or impossibility.

### Coupled conditions

The A2/K2 profile couples producer compromise and modeled contact loss and therefore does not support an unconfounded adversary-only effect claim.

### No global ranking

Condition-specific benefits, equivalence, and disadvantages are preserved. No weighted global score or universal policy rank is reported.

## Primary editorial risks and mitigations

### Risk: perceived cybersecurity-only scope

An editor could view the work as a security paper without sufficient aerospace-information contribution.

**Mitigation:** make the aerospace-specific constraints explicit in the title, abstract, introduction, methods, discussion, and cover letter: intermittent contact, command authority, mission continuity, software recovery, and aerospace mission assurance are part of the experimental decision structure.

### Risk: software-in-the-loop realism

Both studies are controlled software-in-the-loop experiments.

**Mitigation:** emphasize verification/validation value, deterministic reproducibility, frozen protocols, retained provenance, independent Study-2 reproduction, and strict external-validity limitations. Do not imply hardware-in-the-loop, orbital, radio-frequency, or flight validation.

### Risk: article-length pressure

AIAA recommends approximately 10,000–12,000 equivalent words for a Regular/Full Article and counts figure/table space.

**Mitigation:** perform the exact equivalent-word-count audit only after the JAIS export is assembled. Compress editorial exposition rather than deleting frozen limitations or changing results.

### Risk: reference-format conversion

AIAA requires numbered references in citation order and complete DOI-aware bibliographic information.

**Mitigation:** perform reference conversion only on the exact JAIS export, followed by a separate citation-order, DOI, original-source, and completeness audit.

### Risk: artificial-intelligence disclosure mismatch

AIAA requires qualifying artificial-intelligence use to be disclosed in ScholarOne and briefly described in the paper.

**Mitigation:** retain a transparent target-specific disclosure, confirm exact portal wording, and preserve the distinction between publication/reproducibility tooling and deterministic experimental response mechanisms.

### Risk: prior dissertation relationship

The broader research program developed from doctoral work archived through ProQuest.

**Mitigation:** continue transparent disclosure of the dissertation relationship and the additional separately frozen Study-2 work. Do not conceal overlap or describe the journal article as an unchanged dissertation deposit.
