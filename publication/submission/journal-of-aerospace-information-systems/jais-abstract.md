# JAIS Abstract Candidate

**Status:** target-specific candidate for AIAA *Journal of Aerospace Information Systems*.

**Word count:** 189 words by whitespace count.

Satellite cyber response must contain unauthorized activity while preserving mission service and permitting recovery only when current evidence supports a trusted state. We report two separately frozen software-in-the-loop studies of deterministic rule-based response and recovery policies; their statistical populations were analyzed separately. Study 1 included 720 observations across 24 frozen cells. Mission-state dependence was not demonstrated on the predeclared primary outcomes, while modeled missed contact delayed ground-authorized response and reduced policy-visible evidence changed trusted-recovery behavior. Study 2 included 3,872 observations across 85 frozen cells and extended the design to adversarial evidence conditions, multiple contact regimes, context ablations, and bounded producer compromise. Under bounded producer compromise, evidence could remain sufficiently authenticated and current for evidence-qualified recovery while still being false relative to research-only adjudication truth. Contact and authorization conditions produced policy-specific safety and recovery trade-offs, and independent recomputation reproduced all frozen Study 2 numerical results with zero mismatches. Together, the studies show that satellite cyber-response value depends on evidence integrity, authorization, contact availability, and policy context rather than a universal policy ranking. The findings are limited to controlled logical software conditions and do not establish operational flight readiness.
