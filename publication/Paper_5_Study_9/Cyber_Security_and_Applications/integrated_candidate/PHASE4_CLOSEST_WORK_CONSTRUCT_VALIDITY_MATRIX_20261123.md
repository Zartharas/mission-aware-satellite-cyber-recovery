## 3. Construct-validity challenge: strongest rejection case

An editor could reasonably object that the fixed eight-predicate recovery interface asks for trust-registry membership, decision-time freshness/epoch authority, contradiction/completeness, crypto validity, and authorization-path status, none of which the sampled detection datasets were designed to contain. Thus an `all-unknown` outcome and a non-singleton completion set may be almost inevitable. The paper cannot infer that those anomaly-detection benchmarks are poor-quality or unsafe, nor that no mission has such security controls.

**Conservative response:** The purpose is to distinguish a **representation designed for anomaly detection** from an **auditable source of authority for a specific recovery action**. Auditing that distinction across specified releases can remain useful to dataset users designing downstream recovery simulations, provided the contribution is called a finite, contract-relative audit, not a new algorithm or proof of flight resilience. All tested data products are selected source artifacts, not a probability sample of spacecraft.

**Unresolved science:** The published findings do not validate the universality of the eight-predicate contract; no different-contract sensitivity analysis exists; no independent raw-source re-adjudication has occurred; no peer-reviewed editor has assessed the significance. A new scientific study to improve those properties would need its own prospective approval, data rights and design freeze. Reporting these limits is the appropriate revision, rather than inventing favorable results.
