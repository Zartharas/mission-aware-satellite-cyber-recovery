# 6. Conclusion

This article reports two separately frozen software-in-the-loop studies of satellite cyber response and trusted recovery. Study 1 evaluated 720 VALID observations across 24 cells; Study 2 independently evaluated 3,872 VALID observations across 85 cells. The populations were not pooled, and neither study replaced its security, mission, recovery, command-availability, or evidence outcomes with a weighted global policy score.

Study 1 did not demonstrate mission-state dependence on its predeclared P1 primary outcomes. It did show that one modeled missed-contact window imposed an approximately 10-s containment/recovery/state-divergence cost on the ground-authorized P6 path and that controlled omission/reduction of policy-visible evidence materially changed the deterministic mission-aware selector's response and trusted-recovery pathway. Condition-specific Pareto analysis showed benefit, equivalence, mixed performance, and disadvantage rather than universal mission-aware-policy superiority.

Study 2 extended the evidence and contact model under a separate prospective protocol. Its most important security result is a trust-boundary result: under bounded producer compromise, fail-closed and evidence-aware policies could satisfy the evidence-qualified recovery gate in all 96 paired primary observations while the research-only adjudication still classified the unsafe-permissive rate as 1.0. Cryptographically valid/current policy-visible evidence therefore was not equivalent to objective correctness when its producer was inside the adversary budget. Across modeled non-immediate contact, the evidence-aware selector retained an unsafe-permissive rate of 0 by restricting until the next frozen contact opportunity, whereas the fail-closed baseline became initially unsafe-permissive despite later recovery.

The Study-2 context ablations further showed that removing mission, contact, or security information can change security and recovery behavior in scenario-specific ways. The A3 stress profile showed that evidence-qualified recovery can coexist with residual unauthorized state under stronger producer compromise. Conversely, the Block-C benign/adversarial result is intentionally narrow: because the cause label does not change hidden truth or policy-visible evidence, its 54 zero contrasts are a structural label-invariance control rather than evidence about causal fault-versus-attack discrimination.

These results support a bounded cybersecurity and systems-engineering conclusion. Satellite response mechanisms should be evaluated with explicit assumptions about authorization, contact, evidence sufficiency, evidence-producer trust, and recovery qualification. Labels such as mission aware, fail closed, or evidence aware do not guarantee performance. The relevant question is how the implemented mechanism behaves when the information and authority it depends on are incomplete, unavailable, or compromised.

The methodological contribution is therefore a reproducible post-detection response/recovery comparison framework with integrity-frozen empirical records, negative and null results retained, explicit trust boundaries, and independently reproduced Study-2 statistics. The studies do not establish operational flight readiness, RF performance, real operator latency, certification, or a universally preferred autonomous policy.

Future research should remain separately frozen. The next empirical priorities are a genuine causal benign-versus-adversarial ambiguity design, higher-fidelity orbital/access and RF-free hardware-in-the-loop validation, human authorization studies under appropriate approvals, and evidence-plane assurance under key/producer compromise and independent corroboration. Neither the 720-observation Study-1 population nor the 3,872-observation Study-2 population should be expanded post hoc for publication convenience.

Within the controlled research boundary, the central question is not simply whether a spacecraft can respond autonomously to a cyber event. It is whether the response can contain the modeled threat, preserve prioritized service, maintain appropriate command authority, and return to a state whose recovery evidence is both sufficient **and trustworthy under the stated adversary model**.
