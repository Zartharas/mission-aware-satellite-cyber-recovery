**Title - 12 words**  
Satellite Cyber Response and Trusted Recovery Under Contact and Adversarial Evidence Constraints

**Abstract - 171 words**  
Two separately frozen software-in-the-loop studies evaluated deterministic rule-based satellite cyber-response and recovery policies under mission, contact, authorization, and evidence constraints. Study 1 included 720 observations across 24 frozen cells. Mission-state dependence was not demonstrated on the predeclared primary outcomes, whereas modeled missed contact delayed the ground-authorized response path and reduced policy-visible evidence changed trusted-recovery behavior. Study 2 included 3,872 observations across 85 frozen cells and extended the design to adversarial evidence conditions, multiple contact regimes, context ablations, and bounded producer compromise. Under bounded producer compromise, evidence could remain sufficiently authenticated and current for evidence-qualified recovery yet still be false relative to research-only adjudication truth. Contact and authorization conditions produced policy-specific safety and recovery trade-offs, and independent recomputation reproduced all frozen Study 2 numerical results with zero mismatches. The combined evidence shows that satellite cyber-response value depends on evidence integrity, authorization, contact availability, and policy context rather than a universal policy ranking. The findings are limited to controlled logical software conditions and do not establish operational flight readiness, radio-frequency performance, or certification.
