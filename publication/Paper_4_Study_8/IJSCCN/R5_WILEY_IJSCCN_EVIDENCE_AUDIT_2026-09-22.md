## 3. Findings

### 3.1 Scope and article identity

IJSCCN states that it only considers articles with a satellite component. Paper 4 has a central satellite communications and satellite recovery component.

Current 2026 IJSCCN research articles are labelled **Original Paper**, which is the correct target article identity for this submission.

### 3.2 Free Format initial submission

IJSCCN offers Wiley Free Format.

For initial submission:

- the manuscript must be readable and complete;
- references may use any consistent style;
- the manuscript may contain text, figures, and tables together or figures/tables may be separate;
- supporting information is separate;
- Wiley will apply final journal styling after acceptance.

Free Format does not remove journal-specific content limits or metadata requirements.

### 3.3 Required scholarly content does not require literal IMRaD headings

The IJSCCN Free Format text says the manuscript must contain the required scholarly components, including abstract, introduction, methods, results, and conclusions.

This does **not** establish that the literal top-level headings must be exactly “Methods” and “Results.”

Current IJSCCN Original Papers use field-appropriate structures. For example, DOI 10.1002/sat.70062 uses:

- Introduction;
- System Model and Related Works;
- Proposed Interference-Minimized Cell Division;
- Simulation Results;
- Conclusion.

**R5 interpretation:** Paper 4 must make its method, results, and conclusions clearly identifiable. A conventional Methods / Results architecture remains an acceptable editorial choice, but it must not be described as a hard journal heading requirement.

### 3.4 Abstract, keywords, short title, author metadata

IJSCCN requires:

- abstract no longer than 250 words;
- abstract understandable without the rest of the paper;
- no citations to published work in the abstract;
- up to 8 keywords;
- short title up to 70 characters;
- full title, author names, and affiliations entered during submission;
- proof-checking author's full address, email, telephone, and fax details as applicable;
- ORCID for submission;
- biography up to 200 words;
- recent author photograph.

The project remains sole-author: Aman Kumar Singh, MS, PhD. No coauthor is to be added or implied.

Private contact information and photograph remain local-only.

### 3.5 Title page and peer-review model

IJSCCN Free Format asks for a title page with author details and applicable ethics/integrity statements.

The current Wiley Journal Finder record for IJSCCN identifies the journal's peer-review model as **Single Anonymized**. In single-anonymized review, reviewers are anonymous to authors while reviewer access is not based on removing author identity from the submitted manuscript.

Wiley Authors separately explains that a distinct identifying Title Page plus an anonymized main manuscript is used when a journal is configured for double-anonymized review. That generic double-anonymized workflow does not describe the current IJSCCN journal setting.

**R5 decision:** use an identified manuscript by default. Maintain a separate title-page document for submission convenience and metadata completeness, but do not remove author identity from the main manuscript unless the live Research Exchange instance unexpectedly instructs otherwise.

The live Required Files screen still controls whether the title page is uploaded as a separate file and which file designation is used.

### 3.6 Typography and overall layout

IJSCCN prefers:

- 12-point type;
- Times, Helvetica, or Courier;
- double-line spacing is not required.

Free Format means the initial Word file should not imitate final Wiley production typesetting.

**R5 design choice:** professional single-column Word layout, Times New Roman 12 pt body, restrained scholarly heading hierarchy, compact but readable paragraph spacing.

This is a reviewer-readability decision, not a Wiley production template.

### 3.7 Tables: initial-submission ambiguity resolved conservatively

The same IJSCCN page contains two relevant instructions:

- Free Format initial-submission language permits text, figures, and tables in one editable manuscript or as separate files, whichever the author prefers.
- The Manuscript Style section says tables must be on separate pages after the reference list and not incorporated into the main text.
- The Revised Manuscript section explicitly requires tables on separate pages after the reference list.

Therefore, “tables must be separate after References” is unquestionably mandatory at revision, but the initial-submission text creates a genuine journal-page ambiguity.

**R5 decision:** follow the stricter journal-preferred presentation for the R5 initial manuscript by placing tables after References on separate pages, but classify this as a conservative journal-specific presentation choice for initial submission, not an unqualified initial-submission mandate.

### 3.8 Figures: initial submission versus revision

IJSCCN Free Format permits figures to be embedded in the editable initial manuscript or uploaded separately.

The Manuscript Style / Illustrations instructions strongly direct authors to upload figures separately, and revision instructions explicitly require separate figure files.

IJSCCN illustration specifications are more specific than generic Wiley guidance:

- TIFF or EPS;
- photos: 300 dpi;
- graphs/drawings: 800 dpi preferred, 600 dpi minimum;
- mixed photo/drawing artwork: 500 dpi;
- compound figures such as 1a, 1b, 1c should be one figure;
- lettering must remain legible after reduction;
- symbol keys should be in the artwork rather than only the legend;
- tints are not acceptable.

Generic Wiley figure guidance additionally recommends:

- separate original files;
- less than 10 MB per figure;
- figure legends collected in a separate main-text section after References;
- figures cited and numbered in order.

**R5 decision:** prepare separate publication-quality TIFF files from source and also make the reviewer manuscript understandable without depending on external figure files. Do not raster-upsample existing low-resolution images.

### 3.9 Figure legends

A legend is required for each figure.

A dedicated Figure Legends section after References is a Wiley-preferred presentation, not an IJSCCN-specific absolute initial-submission requirement.

**R5 decision:** use a dedicated Figure Legends section because it is professional, reviewer-friendly, and revision-ready.

### 3.10 Graphical Table of Contents

IJSCCN requires a GTOC with the original manuscript.

The GTOC must contain:

- paper title;
- author name(s);
- corresponding author marked with an asterisk;
- an abstract figure;
- no more than 80 words or 3 sentences summarizing the key findings.

The GTOC is a separate submission item and is not Figure 4.

### 3.11 References

Free Format permits any consistent reference format at initial submission.

IJSCCN house style uses:

- numbered citations in square brackets;
- references in order of first appearance;
- complete and accurate records;
- DOI where possible;
- access dates for online references.

**R5 decision:** proactively use the IJSCCN numbered house style to reduce revision work. This is a professionalism choice, not a condition of Free Format acceptance.

### 3.12 Data policy

IJSCCN uses Wiley's **Expects Data Sharing** model:

- data supporting results are expected in an appropriate public repository;
- a Data Availability Statement is required;
- the DAS must describe availability or explain absence;
- when data are shared, the DAS must link to the repository;
- shared data must be cited;
- scripts and analysis artefacts should be publicly archived whenever possible.

Wiley's data guidance defines data broadly enough to include:

- raw and processed data;
- software;
- algorithms;
- protocols;
- methods and related artefacts.

Wiley recommends discipline-specific repositories where possible, otherwise generalist repositories such as Dryad, figshare, or Zenodo.

Zenodo is an example, not a mandatory repository.

### 3.13 DAS placement

There is a Wiley-wide wording difference:

- Wiley Authors submission help says the DAS is entered directly into the submission system and “does not need to be included” in the main manuscript.
- Wiley's Data Sharing FAQ says all Wiley journals require inclusion of a DAS as part of the manuscript.
- IJSCCN itself explicitly requires a DAS and includes it among title-page ethics/integrity statements.

**R5 decision:** include the DAS in the manuscript and enter substantively matching wording in the portal field. This satisfies the stricter journal/data-policy interpretation and avoids omission.

### 3.14 Data citation and persistent identity

Wiley requires formal research-data citation in the reference list.

Recommended form:

[dataset] Authors; Year; Dataset title; Data repository or archive; Version; Persistent identifier.

The manuscript should cite the data/repository at the relevant place in the text and include a formal reference-list entry.

**R5 decision:** use an immutable repository commit/release identity at minimum. A DOI-backed archival record would improve permanence and discoverability but is not required by the public IJSCCN instructions.

### 3.15 Supporting Information

Wiley defines Supporting Information as ancillary scientific material that does not belong in the article itself.

Key points:

- submit as separate files;
- clear labels and legends;
- author is responsible for accuracy/functionality;
- Wiley does not copyedit or alter Supporting Information;
- individual files should generally be 10 MB or less.

Title page, cover letter, author biography, author photograph, GTOC, and publisher declarations are not scientific Supporting Information merely because they are separate files.

When AI substantially edits, develops, or translates manuscript material, transparent disclosure is required. Use solely for spelling, grammar, and general editing is exempt.

Disclosure should identify, as applicable:

- tool;
- purpose;
- whether it influenced key arguments or conclusions;
- how the author reviewed and verified AI-assisted content.

The author remains responsible for all claims, citations, analyses, originality, and final text.

### 3.24 Peer-review readiness

Wiley says reviewers commonly assess:

- originality and significance;
- clarity and conciseness;
- whether conclusions follow from evidence;
- methodological soundness;
- reproducibility and robustness;
- acknowledgement of related and contradictory literature;
- quality and necessity of figures/tables;
- factual, numerical, citation, and unit accuracy;
- ethical compliance.

Common technical/editorial rejection risks include:

- poor preparation;
- missing manuscript elements;
- unreadable figures;
- failure to follow journal guidance;
- out-of-scope work;
- unclear research aim;
- non-reproducible methodology;
- exaggerated conclusions;
- failure to address relevant literature;
- work that looks like an incremental fragment of another paper.

These are reviewer-readiness criteria, not formatting mandates. They should be used as R5 QA gates.
