# Cover Letter — International Journal of Satellite Communications and Networking

Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript **“Post-Quantum Trusted Recovery Under Intermittent Connectivity: Feasibility Across Modeled Contact Budgets and Public Observation-Opportunity Timing”** for publication in the *International Journal of Satellite Communications and Networking*.

The manuscript addresses a satellite-network recovery problem that sits at the intersection of post-quantum cryptographic transition, intermittent communication opportunity, and post-compromise trust restoration. It reports two separately governed finite studies. Study 8 evaluates 3,456 deterministic logical-contact positions under fixed modeled capacity. Study 8E applies the frozen transition requirements to prospectively selected public SatNOGS observation-opportunity timing and solves the minimum hypothetical uniform effective payload rate required for modeled trust restoration. The studies are not statistically pooled and no logical slot is converted to physical time.

The principal result is deliberately non-superlative: the added contact-aware pre-commit guard does not improve the respective feasibility endpoint over ordinary staged cutover in either frozen evidence layer. The manuscript instead shows how standardized cryptographic-object burden, temporal communication opportunity, and recovery horizon constrain modeled feasibility, while transition policy semantics redistribute security-state and availability costs.

The submission is aligned with the journal’s scope in satellite communications and networking, performance analysis, protocols, and systems-oriented research. The paper does not claim operational spacecraft validation, measured link throughput, onboard post-quantum execution performance, or authenticated command-contact availability from SatNOGS observations.

This work is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. The author has no competing interests and reports no external funding. Data, code, frozen protocols, result manifests, and audit records are publicly available in the project repository.

OpenAI ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol) was used for drafting assistance, structural editing, literature-navigation support, and submission-package preparation. The author independently verified the manuscript and all scientific values. The tool was not used to generate or alter the frozen research data or canonical results. A detailed AI Use Declaration is included with the submission materials.
